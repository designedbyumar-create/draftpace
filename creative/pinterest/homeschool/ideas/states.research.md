# states slice: research notes

- Search results gave no real Pinterest data (no pin counts, no saved-search volumes). Results were generic state-by-state pages (HSLDA-style tables, Sonlight, Oakmeadow, Time4Learning, Homeschool.com, Responsible Homeschooling), which confirms the query shape parents use: "homeschool requirements by state" and "homeschool requirements in <State>". Queries in the JSON follow that shape.
- Which states to feature: sources disagree on rankings and note that most states do not count homeschoolers, so counts are only approximate. Raw-count lists name Texas, Florida, California, North Carolina and Georgia among the largest; share-of-students lists put Alaska and North Carolina near the top. No Pennsylvania figure turned up. State cards (Texas, Florida, Pennsylvania, New York, Ohio, plus reserves California and North Carolina) were chosen for population AND for how interesting their data is (Ohio: test and portfolio review are alternatives; New York: individualised plan filed in advance; Pennsylvania: log, portfolio and evaluation all ticked; Texas: None).
- Sources: https://www.insidermonkey.com/blog/10-states-that-have-the-most-homeschoolers-563599 , https://www.carolinajournal.com/nc-ranks-4th-for-the-most-homeschooled-students/ , https://www.kuraplan.com/blog/homeschool-statistics-2026 , https://responsiblehomeschooling.org/state-by-state , https://www.homeschool.com/articles/state-homeschooling-laws/

## Notes for whoever renders these

- Counts verified against src/lib/homeschoolStateRequirements.ts: None 8, Low 15, Moderate 19, High 9 (51 entries). They match the guide's "What the four levels mean".
- The JSON adds one field beyond the brief, `proofExtra`: [{file, proof[]}], for strings that live in another file (the guide's count lines, the four checklist labels in book.ts, the picker text in HouseholdState.tsx, the state page labels in printables/document.tsx). `proof` itself is always grepped against `proofFile` (the data file). All strings were checked with a script; none missing.
- Checklist rows on state cards use the printed record's own labels from book.ts ("A log or record of instruction", "Attendance or hours", "Samples of work or a portfolio", "A test score or evaluation"), ticked per the data's `checklist`. In `items`, "[x]" is ticked and "[ ]" is empty. A tick means the state's note names that thing, not that the state "requires" it; never caption it as a requirement or as compliance.
- The data's own spelling is kept word for word, including British "individualised" (New York note and otherNotes) and "standardised" (North Carolina note). Flagged `source-spelling`. The data file's header says two entries were already corrected for this; these two remain, so consider fixing the source rather than the pin.
- The level definitions on the overview pins are the picker's own HOMESCHOOL_LEVEL_DESCRIPTION strings; they say "typically required" for Moderate and "typically part of the law" for High. Keep "typically" and attribute them to "our summary".
- Real screen exists for the state card: creative/public/screens/homeschooling-companion-kids.png shows "Oregon · Moderate regulation", its note and the caution line (Ava and Leo are demo data; crop the Next.js "N" badge). It does not show the open picker (dropdown, Save, Skip for now).
