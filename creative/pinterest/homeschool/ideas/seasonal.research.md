# seasonal slice: research notes

- Web search returned no Pinterest data (no pin counts, no board names, no search volumes). Every result was a blog, podcast, curriculum-publisher or forum page, so it was used only to choose which moments to cover and which phrases to put in `query`, never for facts on a pin. All words on the pins come from our own guides and book.
- Mid-year reset / January: results framed January as the halfway point of the homeschool year and a natural time to review each curriculum item and rewrite goals; several called the January and February stretch a common low point, and several said to keep the review short and not harshly self-critical. Some suggested a smaller core load; our content has no "one literacy block and one math block" wording, so that was not used. The closest grounded idea is the four-day week (reserve pin). Sources: https://www.homeschool.com/5-ways-to-take-inventory-of-your-homeschool, https://veritaspress.com/blog/your-january-checklist-planning-for-the-second-half-of-the-school-year, https://www.weirdunsocializedhomeschoolers.com/homeschool-reset/
- Mid-semester slump (mid-October to mid-November) and the December break: results called the dip normal and a cue for a gentle reset (fading novelty, shorter days, heavier workload), and said a week or a month off rarely derails a year. This supports pairing the slump with "change one thing, cheapest first" (subject-not-working guide) and the break with the one-evening catch-up (catch-up guide), rather than with anything about motivation. Sources: https://www.rainbowresource.com/blog/the-homeschool-mid-semester-slump/, https://www.freehomeschooldeals.com/restarting-your-homeschool-after-the-holidays/, https://outschool.com/homeschool/homeschool-burnout-mid-year-reset
- Spring evaluation and end of year: results agreed on collecting samples through the year and choosing the best at year-end, a few samples per subject, and booking evaluators early in high-regulation states. They disagreed on how long to keep records (about two years after evaluation up to indefinitely for high school), which matches why our how-long-to-keep guide says to check the state. That is why the June pin carries the guide's own "check your state's own page" line. Sources: https://hslda.org/post/portfolios, https://www.notconsumed.com/?p=311388, https://ourjourneywestward.com/?p=103
- Timing: pins are dated by `when` to the months people would search. With today in early November 2026, the nearest to pin now are the slump pins (still live to late November), the December-break and January pins (publish mid-November to early December so they are indexed before searches rise), and the mid-year-start pins. The spring and June pins can wait until February and April.
- Ungrounded in our content, so deliberately not made: a "back to homeschool" pin built on a planning method (our book is undated and says it "starts whenever you do", which is used), a smaller load of "one core literacy block and one math block", a reminder or "we will nudge you" angle (the product has no reminders), and any claim about which month states require evaluations.
