# Portfolio slice: research notes

Two WebSearch calls. Neither returned any actual Pinterest pins or Pinterest search data, so nothing here is evidence of what pins perform. It is only what portfolio and evaluation pages on the open web emphasise, used to pick angles and query wording.

- Portfolio checklists agree on a core set (log, subject summaries, dated work samples, state-required documents). They say to date every sample, to keep it selective, and to collect work from the start, middle and end of the year. Many "free printable" pages are lead-generation for paid products. This supported a checklist and a two-points-in-the-year comparison, and the query "what to include in a homeschool portfolio". https://heartandsoulhomeschooling.com/homeschool-portfolio-checklist/ and https://homeschoolfox.com/blog/what-goes-into-a-homeschool-portfolio-and-how-to-make-it-shine
- Evaluation pages are mostly evaluator or state-specific. They lead with work samples, a reading list and prior test scores, and say to check with your own evaluator first. This supported the query "homeschool evaluation what to bring" and the "ask your evaluator" line. https://heav.org/?p=316 and https://homeschool-1lf.pages.dev/blog/homeschool-evaluator-massachusetts

Every pin's words and items come from our own guides, the printable's code or the product's record screen, not from these pages. Their per-subject sample counts and "three to five pieces" figures were deliberately not used, because our guide says "Two or three dated pieces per subject".
