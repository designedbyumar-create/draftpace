# Family Health Binder: Pinterest visual strategy

The machine-readable version of this brief is `family-health-binder.json` in this folder. It covers 35 Draftpace pins (waves 2–5, `health/pin-4.1` to `health/pin-10.5`) and 10 Maple & Main pins (`Pin-mm-family-health-binder-01` to `-10`).

## The problem with the current pins

Right now every product uses the same finds-pin template: a serif headline, a few of the shared flat icons (`doctor`, `pills`, `notebook`, `health`), and a product card. A health binder pin ends up looking like a travel pin with a pill bottle on it. These archetypes swap the shared icons for this product's own world. That world is the family kitchen, not a clinic: a tabbed binder on the table, the fridge door, the medicine cabinet, the intake counter and the camp check-in table. The pin also shows the product's real printed pages.

## Who pins this

- The parent who fills in every school, camp and sports form, and keeps everyone's allergies, medications and insurance numbers in their head.
- The sandwich-generation caregiver, with kids' papers in one pile and a parent's pharmacy bag in another.
- The parent at an intake desk trying to remember exactly when the fever started.
- The parent leaving kids with a sitter or grandparent.
- People who already save "family medical binder", "medication list printable", "emergency contact sheet" and "questions to ask your doctor" pins.

## What wins in this niche

- **Show the actual page.** Pinterest's guidance for product sellers is to put the product front and centre, use a 2:3 pin (1000×1500), keep copy short, and keep the logo subtle and out of the lower-right corner, where Pinterest overlays its icons ([Pinterest Business Community](https://community.pinterest.biz/t/the-best-pin-formats-and-practices-for-product-sellers/1319); [SocialKit](https://socialk.it/en/blog/pinterest-pin-design-best-practices)). Printable sellers lead with styled mockups of the page and make several pin variations per product ([Dodo Payments](https://dodopayments.com/blogs/sell-printables-online)). Clipboard, paper and binder mockup kits are sold for exactly this, with binder mockups shown open, closed and lying flat ([Creative Market binder mockup](https://creativemarket.com/yogurt86/291639830-Binder-Mock-up)).
- **Binders mean tabs and sections.** The free and paid medical binders that rank in this niche are sold as sets of sections: dividers, medication log, symptom tracker, family history ([Sarah Titus](https://www.sarahtitus.com/medical-binder/)). Others list allergies, primary doctor, medications, symptoms and immunizations ([Kiddycharts](https://www.kiddycharts.com/printables/family-medical-binder/)). Some recommend tabs in coordinating colours for each family member ([Mom Colored Glasses](http://momcoloredglasses.com/healthy-living/family-medical-binder-easy-organize-familys-health-information/)) or organising sections with binder tabs ([Family Caregivers Online](https://familycaregiversonline.net/caregiver-education/how-to-build-a-medical-binder/)). The pin format in search is the sample-page preview ([example pin](https://www.pinterest.com/pin/804174077202564820/)).
- **Emergency "fridge sheets" and caregiver packs are their own category.** Packs include an emergency fridge sheet next to medication and appointment trackers ([Payhip pack](https://payhip.com/b/YNFIn)). Caregiver binders lead with a grab-and-go emergency sheet, a medical team directory and a medication tracker ([Etsy caregiver binder](https://www.etsy.com/listing/4509768587)).
- **Numbered questions and checklists.** Doctor-visit worksheets use a header and numbered question lines ([Steward Medical Group worksheet](https://content.steward.org/sites/default/files/2021-10/SMG%20Doctor%20Appointment%20Worksheet.pdf)), and question lists are short enough to print and take along ([InformedHealth](https://www.informedhealth.org/list-of-questions/)).
- **Text works as the headline: bold, legible, uncrowded.** Script fonts read badly on mobile, so keep important words in a clear face, and keep text clear of the bottom-right corner ([Tailwind](https://www.tailwindapp.com/blog/pinterest-pin-design-tips)). Design at 1000×1500, and expect a pin to keep working for months ([Schedulala](https://schedulala.com/blog/pinterest-pin-design-best-practices)). Asking for the save on the pin helps ([Pinterest community](https://community.pinterest.biz/t/tips-for-encouraging-more-saves-on-your-pins/984)). Older repin research found taller images drew more repins ([Ragan](https://www.ragan.com/infographic-how-to-get-more-repins-on-pinterest/)).

Limits of this research: this environment could not open pinterest.com, etsy.com or the Pinterest community site directly. The findings above come from search-result summaries of those pages. No source gave per-format save or click data for this niche, so the format choices are a judgement from what the niche's top sellers and guides show, not measured rates.

## Look and feel

Rose on warm paper, the product's own theme (`#b23a5b`, soft `#f9e3ea`, ground `#faf6f3`, ink `#241a1b`). People and tabs get the product's own person colours (`PERSON_HUES`), which label a person and never signal anything about their health. Magnets and sticky notes get one warm pop colour (butter or peach). There is no clinical blue-and-white, no medical red and no red cross. Privacy pins never show a padlock: the product's privacy setting is a print filter, not a lock. The drawn pages show only real field labels, and filled-in values are grey strokes, not invented handwriting.

## The seven archetypes

**1. Tabbed binder on the kitchen table (`binder-flatlay`).** A top-down open binder on wood or linen, with readable tabs down the edge. The tab labels are real: the printed book's own sections (Family member, Medical facts, Symptom log, Care team, Immunizations, Visit, Emergency card, Caregiver sheet), the relationship labels (Child, Parent, You), or the five printable names. This is the image a "family medical binder" searcher expects, so it reads as the thing they were looking for. Used for binder ideas, the care team, two-generation binders and "5 printables".

**2. The printed page, up close (`page-closeup`).** One of the five real printed pages, or a blank book page, tilted on a clipboard, at the intake counter or next to a school form. One real line is highlighted: the tick boxes, the "left off" footer, or the stopped medication. This is the mockup-first format printable sellers rely on, and it lets someone judge the sheet before they click. Used for most shop pins about a specific page.

**3. On the fridge, in the wallet (`fridge-and-wallet`).** A fridge door with the Emergency card or Caregiver sheet under a magnet, a wallet with the card peeking out, and scissors on the dashed cut line. It tells the viewer where the page lives, which is what makes an emergency printable feel worth saving. Used for emergency cards and sheets, the sitter sheet, and "keep it where you can find it".

**4. What to write, numbered (`numbered-list`).** Large numbered badges, each with one small drawing and one line copied from the linked guide's own ordered list or timeline. These are the niche's most-saved guide pins ("what to include", "what to bring"). Used for guide-linked pins.

**5. The medicine cabinet and the list (`medicine-cabinet`).** An open cabinet with blank-label bottles (no drug names, no doses) and the medication list taped inside the door. The list's corner carries the real "This list is right today" / "Checked today" stamp or a "Stopped" fold. It shows the guide's core habit, checking the list against the bottles. Used for medication pins. A pill organiser may appear only as set dressing, with no day letters, because the product has no schedule or reminders.

**6. One record, many readers (`page-fan`).** Three to five pages or person cards fanned out, each with a small object for who receives it: a house key for the sitter, a backpack for school or camp, a stethoscope for a new doctor. It works like a "what's included" pin and shows the product's real point of difference: different pages for different people. Used for privacy and "what to leave off" pins, the five printables, and "a card per person".

**7. From the drawer of printouts to one page (`pile-to-page`).** A before/after layout. The top shows the listing's own problem ("a kitchen drawer of printouts"): sticky notes, two paper stacks, scattered cards. The bottom shows one clean page, or the real app screenshot in a phone. This is where the real screenshots (members, workspace) earn their place. Used for "typed once", POV, camp check-in and caregiver pins.

## Things to watch when rendering

- There are no screenshots of the Symptoms or Visits screens. Those pins use the drawn printed pages, built from the real labels, rather than a phone.
- In `family-health-binder-members.png`, the bottom navigation covers part of the Medications row. Crop to the "Not checked yet / This list is right today" panel.
- A few pins quote rows from a guide's invented example (the questions pile in pin 4.3, the dated symptom lines in pin 6.4, the sitter / camp / doctor table in pin 9.1). The guides label these rows as examples, so the pin needs a small "Example" tag. Pin 10.3 uses the labels Child and Parent instead of the guide's invented names.
- Never show a red cross, a padlock, a bell or clock suggesting reminders, or a pill organiser with days or times. The product does none of these things.
