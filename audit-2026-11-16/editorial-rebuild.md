# Editorial rebuild: decision framework

This is the framework for acting on the individual audit, not a claim that all article reviews are complete.

## Research before commissioning

For each URL, define one reader, one situation, one geography when relevant, and one outcome. Record the query evidence separately: GSC page impressions establish exposure, query reports establish observed wording, community discussions establish qualitative questions, and keyword tools can estimate market volume. None substitutes for another. No monthly volume estimates are available in this audit.

Keep a source ledger for each consequential claim, including exact supporting passage, source date, jurisdiction, and verification date. A generic source list at the bottom does not establish support for every claim. Dates marked 'checked' must reflect an actual review.

## Reader-first structure

Lead with the answer and the conditions under which it applies. Let the reader choose their situation only when the steps actually differ. Present actionable steps, a worked example, exceptions, and a usable resource. Avoid repeating the same answer in an introduction, timeline, list, and FAQ without adding information.

Choose the resource to match intent: a printable checklist for checklist searches, a worked calculation for affordability or scheduling, a comparison for choices, and exact contact steps or scripts for administrative problems. Basic help must work without buying a product.

## Visual policy

Use images only when they explain, demonstrate, document, or help the reader identify something. A photographed shutoff valve with annotations is useful; a generic room image is not a substitute. Use real interface screenshots for product instructions and labelled fictional data for examples. Do not imply a fictional case is firsthand experience. Do not infer AI provenance from appearance alone.

Tables should generally remain selectable, responsive HTML rather than raster illustrations duplicating the same table. Provide actual downloadable or printable resources where the search intent calls for them. Test small-screen legibility and print output.

## Content architecture

Use practical subject hubs with situational entry points: urgent problem, decision, initial setup, ongoing upkeep. Preserve established article URLs where practical. Merge only where the articles answer the same intent and evidence supports consolidation; related subjects alone do not justify redirects. Distinguish country/state-specific content explicitly and avoid linking a country-specific reader to incompatible instructions without warning.

## Priority rules

1. Correct verified harmful or materially misleading claims first, regardless of traffic.
2. Fix inaccessible pages and broken promised resources.
3. Improve articles with observed search exposure and clear intent mismatch.
4. Consolidate demonstrated duplication after reviewing query and link evidence.
5. Commission new subjects only after documenting unmet need and a credible contribution.

## Measurement

Record publication/change dates and compare page/query exposure over comparable periods. Track useful resource actions and product outcomes separately from search clicks. Treat low-impression CTR as uncertain. Check indexing and canonical changes after mergers. Never promise rankings or attribute changes solely to an edit without considering seasonality, indexing, and search-result changes.
