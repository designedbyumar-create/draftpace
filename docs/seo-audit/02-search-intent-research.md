# Search intent research

Real web searches run during this pass, with what each one confirmed. Everything below is either (a) a live search result from this session, (b) a direct quote/fact from an official source surfaced by that search, or (c) explicitly marked as a hypothesis. No search volume, keyword difficulty, or traffic forecast is stated anywhere in this document, because none of it is available without a keyword-research tool this environment does not have.

## 1. Florida homeschool notice of intent (guide: `homeschool-notice-of-intent-explained`)

**Claim in the guide:** filed with the district superintendent, within 30 days of starting, for a child six or older by February 1 of the school year, once rather than annually.

**Verified against:** the Florida Senate's current (2025) statute text for s. 1002.20, searched directly.

**Result: confirmed accurate.** The compulsory-attendance cutoff really is age 6 by February 1, home education is a recognized way to satisfy it, and the filing-once (vs. New Mexico's annual renewal) distinction holds. No change made.

## 2. New Mexico homeschool registration (same guide)

**Claim in the guide:** registered within 30 days of starting, renewed annually between June 1 and August 1, filed with the state (not the district), online or on paper.

**Verified against:** NMPED's own notification form and statute summary (NMSA 22-1-2.1), surfaced directly by search.

**Result: confirmed accurate**, down to the specific renewal window. One additional real fact surfaced that the guide does not mention: NM also requires instructor qualification (a high school diploma or equivalent) and immunization records. This is a true additional requirement, but it is outside this guide's stated scope (it is specifically about the notice of intent, not full compliance), so this is noted here rather than forced into the guide as scope creep.

## 4. Symptom log for a doctor visit — competing search results

**Query run:** symptom diary / symptom log, what to write down before an appointment.

**What ranks:** hospital-system health libraries (WebMD, St. Luke's, University of Michigan Health, Houston Methodist, ColumbiaDoctors) and a doctor-visit prep service (Ubie).

**What they cover that Draftpace's guide does not:** trigger/influence tracking (activity, stress, skipped meals), medication timing relative to the symptom, and a suggested 1-2 week logging period before the visit.

**Why no change was made:** Draftpace's guide deliberately uses four fields (onset, duration, severity, what helped) because that is what Family Health Binder's actual structured symptom timeline supports. Recommending additional fields the product does not capture would misrepresent what the product does, which the task brief explicitly prohibits ("do not invent functionality"). This is a deliberate scope boundary, not a content gap, and the guide already says so ("A notes page, not a symptom checker").

## 5. Questions to bring to the doctor — competing search results

**Query run:** how to prepare a list of questions for a doctor appointment.

**What ranks:** Zocdoc, Informed Health (a German public-information service translated for international audiences), Mount Sinai, Better Health Victoria.

**Key pattern found:** multiple sources independently recommend ranking by importance and keeping the list to a handful of questions (Informed Health specifically suggests up to six). Draftpace's guide already does exactly this ("one question per line, the important one first... keep the list to what a short visit can hold"). No gap found.

## 6. IRS $1,000 estimated-tax threshold (guide: `how-to-budget-with-irregular-income`)

**Verified against:** IRS Form 1040-ES guidance and the Taxpayer Advocate Service's 2026 estimated-payments guidance, surfaced directly.

**Result: confirmed accurate**, including that the threshold is measured after withholding and that the 90%/100%(110%) safe-harbor test applies alongside it. The guide's own framing ("generally need to... if you expect to owe $1,000 or more... spread over four payment periods") matches. No change made.

## 7. UK executor's role — researched fresh for the enrichment in deliverable 5

Three separate, specific claims were verified against primary UK government/legislative sources before being added to `named-executor-what-you-agreed-to-uk`:

- **Renunciation mechanics** (form PA15, "power reserved" for a co-executor who does not want to act now, the original-will-not-a-photocopy requirement) — confirmed against GOV.UK's "Applying for probate" guidance.
- **Letters of administration for intestacy** — confirmed against HMRC's own Inheritance Tax Manual (IHTM05101), a primary source, not a law-firm summary.
- **Lay executors generally cannot charge for their own time, unlike a professional executor, unless the will has a charging clause** — confirmed against the Trustee Act 2000 itself (Part V, sections 28-29) on legislation.gov.uk, the actual statute text, not a secondary description of it.

All three are now cited in the guide's `sources` array with the real URLs above.
